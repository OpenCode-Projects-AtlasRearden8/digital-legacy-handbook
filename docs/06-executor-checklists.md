# Executor-Friendly Checklists

*If you're reading this because someone you love died: I'm sorry, and I'm glad
you have this folder. You don't have to do everything this week — or even this
month. Do today's list, be kind to yourself the rest of the day, and come back
tomorrow.*

*If you're reading it because you're preparing your own folder: this doubles
as a preview of what your trusted person will face — everything you organize
today removes a line below.*

---

## What "executor" means here (plain English)

The **executor** (also called a *personal representative* or, if there's no
will, an *administrator*) is the person a court authorizes to carry out the
estate's business: paying what's owed from the estate, closing accounts,
transferring what remains. You usually get official papers after a court or
probate process — the **executor certificate** or **letter of
administration**. Until you have those papers, almost every "official
channel" task (closing accounts, estate bank) waits — but the checklists
below include plenty you can do without them. The [glossary](08-glossary.md)
has full definitions.

## Checklist 0 — The first 72 hours (no papers needed)

- [ ] Find and read the handoff letter and this folder
- [ ] Secure the phone (don't factory-reset!) and laptop; note where the
      password manager lives
- [ ] Locate key-originals: will, deed, insurance policies (per the location
      key in the folder)
- [ ] Arrange care for dependents — pets, plants, the FFXIV guild raids can
      wait, the golden retriever can't
- [ ] Wash, eat, sleep. Seriously — this list will still be here.

## Checklist 1 — Week one: stop the bleeding

- [ ] Get **official death certificates** — ask for 6–10 copies when first
      issued (every institution wants its own; that's normal and not a scam)
- [ ] Walk the statements, per [closing recurring
      charges](05-closing-recurring-charges.md) — list and stop them
- [ ] Freeze/lock payment cards that shouldn't keep charging (keep-list first)
- [ ] Notify employer/HR; ask about final pay, benefits, life-insurance
      enrollment details
- [ ] Set mail forwarding or have someone collect mail — statements arriving
      = list of who else to notify

## Checklist 2 — Paperwork phase (weeks to months)

- [ ] Learn who's legally in charge (will → probate → court names executor;
      no will → court appoints administrator). An attorney consult is worth
      it; many probate courts have free help desks
- [ ] Open (if needed) an **estate bank account** — the legal channel through
      which the estate pays and receives money
- [ ] Contact institutions one at a time with your papers: banks, brokerages,
      credit cards, utilities. Log contact name + date each call
- [ ] Close or memorialize digital accounts per the [wishes
      list](04-memorialize-or-delete.md); export photos and documents *before*
      closing anything
- [ ] Identify all assets per the master list in this folder; note anything
      that **doesn't apply** or is already handled (a spouse's name on it,
      e.g.) so future-you knows you checked it

## Checklist 3 — Digital cleanup (once stable)

- [ ] Export and copy: photos, personal documents, tax-relevant mail, and
      anything sentimental *first* (twice — to two locations)
- [ ] Then, per the wishes list: memorialize, delete, or transfer each account
- [ ] Devices: wipe/reset only after exports; keep the laptop unformatted
      until taxes and the estate's final accounting are done
- [ ] One year later: one final "did anything still bill?" statement sweep
- [ ] Close the email account only when *everything* is done, and tell the
      family how to reach the archive if they ever want it

## Checklist 4 — Close out, gently

- [ ] Final tax filings including the estate's (an accountant for this one)
- [ ] Distribute per the will once debts and taxes are settled (not before —
      "pay debts first" is the rule that keeps executors out of trouble)
- [ ] Keep your own records of everything for at least a few years
- [ ] Do something kind for yourself. Being someone's executor is work, and
      you kept your promise to them.
