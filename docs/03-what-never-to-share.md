# What Never to Share

*This handbook is mostly about writing things down. This page is about the
five things you should treat like medicine: locked up, labeled, and never left
lying around. Respecting this list is what keeps your kindness from becoming
someone else's breach.*

---

## The five never-in-plain-sight items

### 1. Raw passwords in a document that travels

Example of what **not** to write: `Gmail — hunter2!` on a page.

Better: `Gmail — password lives in my password manager, entry "Email main."
Printed emergency kit for that manager is in the blue envelope inside this
folder.`

Why: paper folders get seen, moved, photographed, and copied — by contractors,
guests, kids, and inevitably by regret. A password you can't change for a dead
person is the worst kind of exposure. A *pointer* to a secured secret is safe;
a raw secret on paper is not.

### 2. Seed phrases / recovery codes / backup codes

Crypto seed phrases, one-time backup codes, account recovery keys — these are
the absolute crown jewels, because **they bypass passwords entirely**.

Rule: never write these on the same page or the same file as anything else.
If you must preserve them:

- Store them in a password manager's secure notes, **or**
- On paper, inside a separate sealed envelope labeled only with a neutral
  word, stored in a different physical location (a safe, a safe-deposit box),
  with a note in your folder: *"Recovery envelope — separate location, see
  location key."*

Never photograph them. Never put them in a cloud note. Never text them.

### 3. Your master password, even to your trusted person — usually

Better pattern: **two-envelope split.**

- Envelope A (inside the folder): *"The password hint sheet"* — hints, not
  the password.
- Envelope B (with the lawyer, or a bank safe-deposit box, or a truly
  separate location): the password itself, sealed.

Nobody can rob your desk drawer and get in with just A. Your trusted person
with A + knowledge of B is fine. If your situation is simple and low-risk
(joint accounts, one email), telling your trusted person the master password
by voice — never in writing — is a reasonable middle ground.

### 4. Government numbers and full financial details in one place

Don't collect Social Security numbers, full card numbers, and full account
numbers all on one sheet. That page is a ***raid kit*** — everything identity
thieves need in one photo.

You almost never need full numbers for the tasks in this handbook. Write
"institution + last 4" instead; institutions verify by more than the number
anyway, and your executor will work with them directly.

### 5. Anything that shouldn't be found at all

Private journals, health details you haven't chosen to share, someone else's
secrets you were told in confidence, the sort of notes that are private only
because they're private.

Your folder will one day be read by exactly the people you named — but folders
get opened in emergencies by whoever is standing there. If it would hurt or
embarrass that moment, keep it **somewhere else**. This is not about hiding
truth; it's about not making a hard day harder and distracting from the
practical jobs.

## Fine to share (you can relax about these)

- Where documents and passwords **live** (pointers)
- Institution names, last-4s, and what each account is *for*
- Wishes, plain as day — kindness needs no password
- Contacts and who-to-call order
- The contents of the [handoff letter](07-handoff-letter.md)

---

## A quick test for anything you're unsure about

Ask three questions:

1. **If a stranger photographed this page, what could they do?** (Bad answer
   = log in, drain, lock, or hold things hostage.)
2. **Could my trusted person complete their real jobs without it?** Usually:
   yes, with a pointer instead of the secret.
3. **Is this stored in its own stronger layer** (manager, sealed envelope,
   separate location)? If it's a crown jewel, it needs one.

Two "yes anything bad" answers → move it to the stronger layer, keep the
pointer.

## And once: check the back doors you didn't mean to leave open

- Phone: lock screen notifications for texts and email — turn previews off so
  codes don't show on a locked screen
- Laptop: full-disk encryption on (FileVault / BitLocker / built-in
  alternatives); fix "would anyone guess this login?" now
- Old devices and drives in drawers: factory-reset or wipe anything you
  wouldn't hand to a stranger with the data on it
- Browser: turn off "save passwords in plain browser" if your only copy of
  them is there and unprotected
