# Glossary

*Every term in this handbook, explained like a friend would. Bookmark this
page; it's short on purpose. When you eventually meet with an attorney or
accountant, knowing these words turns a $400/hour meeting into a $200/hour
meeting.*

---

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ACH** | The network banks use to pull money directly out of accounts (like autopay for rent or insurance). Not a card charge; a bank draft. |
| **Administrator** | What the court calls an "executor" when someone dies **without** a will. Same job, fancier paperwork. |
| **Advance directive** | A legal document saying what medical care you do or don't want if you can't speak for yourself. Often paired with a medical power of attorney. |
| **Beneficiary** | The person (or people) a financial account or insurance policy automatically pays when you die. Payable-on-death and transfer-on-death designations are the same idea. |
| **Chargeback** | Asking your card company to reverse a charge. Useful if a merchant keeps billing a cancelled service. |
| **Deposit box (safe-deposit box)** | A little locked box inside a bank's vault you rent, for truly original documents. Note its location in your folder or nobody will find it. |
| **Digital legacy** | This entire handbook's subject: the plan for your accounts, data, and wishes after you can't manage them yourself. |
| **Digital twin** | Your folder's encrypted digital copy, stored somewhere else. |
| **Encryption** | Turning files into unreadable code unless someone has the password (or key). A password-protected ZIP is entry-level encryption. |
| **Estate** | Everything a person owns at death, and the process by which it is counted, settled, and passed on. |
| **Estate bank account** | A legal account in the estate's name that pays the estate's bills once you have executor papers. Executors shouldn't pay estate bills from personal accounts. |
| **Executor** | The person named in a will (and confirmed by a court) to carry out the estate's business. Also called a personal representative. |
| **Executor certificate** | The official paper a court gives you (the will's executor or the estate's administrator) proving you may act for the estate. Different states name this differently — letters testamentary, letters of administration, letter of authority — but they all mean "you're officially in charge." |
| **Grace period** | A window (often days to a couple months) before late fees kick in. Always ask for yours; you almost always get a real answer. |
| **Inactive Account Manager** | Google's built-in feature that shares or deletes your data after you stop using the account, with people you choose. |
| **Intestate** | Dying without a valid will. It's not a disaster — states have default rules — but it makes the process slower and less in your control, which is reason enough to make a will. |
| **Legacy contact** | A named person a platform trusts to act for you (Apple, Meta). Set it while you're alive; it takes minutes. |
| **Letter of administration** | Court paperwork that makes you the legal administrator when there's no will. See "executor certificate." |
| **Master key principle** | The handbook's rule: email + phone are master keys to everything else; tend to them first. |
| **Memorialization** | A platform turning a profile into a locked, visitable memorial page (friends can grieve, no logins please). |
| **Next of kin** | Closest living relative(s) by law. Matters mostly when there's no will and for medical decisions. |
| **Password manager** | A piece of software (some free, some paid, all of them local or synced by design) that stores all your passwords behind one master password. |
| **POA (Power of attorney)** | A legal document authorizing someone to act for you while you're alive. Ends at death — then executor/estate authority takes over. |
| **Personal representative** | Formal name for "executor" in many states. Same job. |
| **Probate** | The court process that confirms a will and authorizes an executor. Can be quick or slow; ask the local court's help desk. |
| **Pro-rated refund** | A partial refund for the unused time in a paid period. Ask annually-billed merchants; sometimes it happens. |
| **Recurring charge** | Any charge that repeats automatically (subscriptions, autopay). See [closing recurring charges](05-closing-recurring-charges.md). |
| **Secure note** | A password manager feature for storing sensitive text, not just logins. Where seed phrases/backup codes should live. |
| **Seed phrase** | A one-time, write-it-once word list that controls a crypto wallet (or similar). Never photographed, never emailed, never in your main folder. |
| **Statement sweep** | The executor move of reading bank/card statements to find what actually charges. The most reliable list of recurring charges that exists. |
| **Two-envelope split** | This handbook's pattern for master passwords: hints in the folder, the secret itself elsewhere. |
| **Will** | The legal document naming your executor and saying where your things go. Doesn't need to be fancy; it needs to exist. |
