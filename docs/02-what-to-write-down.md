# What to Write Down (Accounts, Wishes, Passwords)

*The goal of this page: a single list your loved one can read top to bottom on
the worst day without you, and do the right thing at every line. Write like
you're texting a friend who is tired and crying. Short lines. Bold the stuff
they'll need first.*

---

## The master list (copy this into your folder)

### 1. Essential access — the keys to everything

List **where keys live**, not usually the keys themselves (see
[what never to share](03-what-never-to-share.md)):

- [ ] Phone passcode or unlock method
- [ ] Computer login
- [ ] Password manager: name, where it's installed, master password location
      (from memory? hint sheet? emergency kit printout?)
- [ ] Email accounts — list **all** of them; email is the master key for
      password resets, so this is the most important line on the whole page
- [ ] Where the encrypted backup of this folder lives, if any
      (plus who holds that password — never in the same place as the file)
- [ ] Apple ID / Google ID / Microsoft ID — noting **realm access** (legacy
      contact, recovery email, trusted phone) exists for each

### 2. Money — where the dollars live

- [ ] Bank accounts and credit unions — name the bank, "checking/savings,"
      whether it's joint or solo, where statements come
- [ ] Credit cards (especially any with automatic payments)
- [ ] Investments, retirement accounts, HSA
- [ ] Life insurance — **policy number location**, not usually the policy
- [ ] Mortgage, auto loan, student loans — lender names
- [ ] Recurring charges (next section) — the [cancellation
      playbook](05-closing-recurring-charges.md) covers how to stop them

### 3. Paper trail — where originals live

- [ ] Will / trust — where originals are, who drafted it, attorney name
- [ ] Advance directive / medical power of attorney location
- [ ] ID documents (birth certificate, passport, Social Security card)
- [ ] Car title, house deed, lease
- [ ] Insurance policies (home, auto, health, life) and agent phone numbers

### 4. People — who to call, in what order

- [ ] Executor / personal representative name and contact
- [ ] Attorney, accountant, financial advisor names
- [ ] Employer / HR contact
- [ ] One or two family or friends trusted to coordinate
- [ ] Clergy or community contacts if relevant

### 5. Digital property — the online stuff

- [ ] Social media accounts (and your [wishes](04-memorialize-or-delete.md)
      for each)
- [ ] Photos — *where* the collection actually lives (phone only? cloud?
      a hard drive in the closet that matters?)
- [ ] Domain names, websites, GitHub, substack, portfolios — anything someone
      might want to renew, archive, or delete
- [ ] Subscriptions with content (Kindle purchases, audiobooks, games — most
      of these **don't transfer**, which is good to know in advance)
- [ ] Business / side-gig logins — location only, plus "who to tell"

### 6. Wishes — short and plain

One paragraph is plenty:

- Social media: **memorialize, keep, or delete** — per account
- Devices and accounts: who should close what, and roughly when
- Anything that matters to people: photos, files, a journal app, FFXIV guild
  you'd want notified — small things are allowed and welcome

## The wishes page, filled as an example (fictional)

> **My wishes, short version**
> Social: memorialize my account (I set it up years ago); let Aunt Bea's son
> take over the chess club page by inviting them early.
> Delete: streaming, rideshare, delivery apps once things settle.
> Keep: photos (they're all on my phone and also at my Photos backup);
> documents on the laptop.
> Printer/binder: Aunt Bea has the memorabilia folder I'd like shared.

Names above are fictional examples — that's the right way to write yours:
specific, short, no legal language.

## Format tips that make it friend-lovely

- Use the same phrases as this handbook ("location *not* password") so your
  reader can cross-reference guides
- Number every section; your reader may read only #1 and #2 that day and that's
  fine — they should still be able to act
- Put the [**handoff letter**](07-handoff-letter.md) on top; this list behind it

## Common mistakes (avoid these four)

1. **Writing passwords in the list.** Store secrets separately and note
   locations instead (see [what never to share](03-what-never-to-share.md)).
2. **Listing accounts nobody actually uses.** Skip the app you opened in 2019
   and never touched — or better, delete those now (fewer lines!).
3. **Assuming "they'll figure it out."** They won't, kindly, on a Tuesday at
   11 pm. Write the phone passcode location now.
4. **Never telling anyone the folder exists.** Folder + one trusted person =
   complete. Either alone is unfinished.
