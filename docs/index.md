# Digital Legacy Handbook — Start Here

*This handbook exists so the people you love never have to guess. It's written
in plain English, step by step, and you don't need to finish it in one sitting.
Ten minutes a week is enough to make a real difference.*

---

## Why this exists

If something happened to you tomorrow — a hospital stay, a sudden accident, or
the worst day — the people who care about you would have two hard jobs:

1. Getting through it emotionally.
2. Finding out **what bills are due, which accounts matter, and where anything
   lives** — often with no permission, no passwords, and no map.

The second job steals time and energy from the first. This handbook is how you
take that second job off their shoulders now, while it's easy, while nobody is
panicking, and while you still remember which email you used for the car
insurance.

It's not morbid. It's the same kindness as leaving spare keys with a neighbor:
most likely you'll just be the one who finds them handy next Tuesday.

## How to use this handbook

Work through the guides in order, or jump to what you need:

- [Build the when-I'm-gone folder](01-when-im-gone-folder.md) — the physical
  (or encrypted digital) folder everything else lives in.
- [What to write down](02-what-to-write-down.md) — accounts, wishes,
  passwords, and the checklist that organizes them.
- [What to never share](03-what-never-to-share.md) — a few things that should
  *not* go in the folder, even from family.
- [Memorialize or delete](04-memorialize-or-delete.md) — what happens to your
  online accounts and how to choose.
- [Closing recurring charges](05-closing-recurring-charges.md) — stopping
  bills, subscriptions, and autopay for a loved one.
- [Executor checklists](06-executor-checklists.md) — printable, step-by-step
  lists for the person who will handle things.
- [Handoff letter](07-handoff-letter.md) — a one-page letter you can print and
  sign. The single most important page in the whole project.
- [Glossary](08-glossary.md) — plain-English definitions for every term used
  here.

## The three golden rules

1. **Write it down, but not the secrets themselves.** Record *where* things
   are and *how to get to them* — not raw passwords pasted into a page.
   (Details and exceptions in [what never to share](03-what-never-to-share.md).)
2. **Tell one trusted person the folder exists** and where it is. A folder
   nobody knows about helps nobody.
3. **Review it once a year.** Put a recurring reminder on your calendar:
   *"Digital legacy review — 20 minutes."* Update awkward parts, toss stale
   ones, done.

## A promise about privacy

Nothing in this handbook requires you to trust any company, app, or website —
including us. It works on paper, in a locked drawer, with no cloud at all.
Everything stays **local-first**: your words, printed or stored where *you*
choose.

If you do keep a digital copy, keep it **encrypted** (a password-protected ZIP
or a password manager's secure notes — both are in the guides), and never
email it to anyone as-is.

## It's education, not legal advice

Estate law, inheritance, and who's allowed to touch whose email vary a lot by
state and country. This handbook gives you the practical scaffolding; for
anything high-stakes (wills, trusts, business accounts), a local attorney is
worth the hour. The [glossary](08-glossary.md) marks every legal term so you
can walk into that meeting already knowing the vocabulary.
