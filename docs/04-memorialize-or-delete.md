# Memorialize or Delete? Deciding What Happens to Each Account

*When someone dies, their accounts go one of three ways: they keep running
forever like a ghost, they're memorialized (a gentle frozen state most big
platforms offer), or they're deleted. Deciding this for yourself in advance —
and knowing how to do it for someone else — is one of the kindest things in
this handbook.*

---

## The three states, plainly

**1. Ghost (do nothing).** The account sits there. Birthday reminders keep
popping for friends. Hackers eventually probe dormant accounts. Spammers use
old addresses. This is the default, and the default is usually the worst
choice. Avoid it by deciding on purpose.

**2. Memorialized (remember mode).** Most large platforms offer this:
 the profile is locked, no new posts, existing friends can still visit and
grieve. nuisance: birthday reminders stop, and nobody can log in as the
person. This is right when the account held connection — social media mainly.

**3. Deleted (closed).** Data is removed per the service's policy — some
immediately, some after a wait, some after you export first. This is right
for utilitarian accounts (rides, streaming, shopping) and anything with a
subscription attached to it.

*Email special note:* keep one primary email alive (a family member manages it)
long enough to catch: password resets for other accounts, invoices, insurance
statements, and any messages people still send. Deleting email too fast is the
classic executor mistake — it locks everything else out.

## Set it up while you're here (2 minutes each, once)

These are the steps you'd otherwise leave to your executor; doing them now is
a form of kindness. Names of menus change; search inside each app's settings
for the term in **bold**:

- **Apple:** Settings → your name → **Legacy Contact** — pick someone; they get
  an access key when the time comes.
- **Google:** myaccount.google.com → **Inactive Account Manager** — name a
  trusted person and a wait time; Google notifies and shares what you allow.
- **Microsoft:** there is a notarized next-of-kin process (search "Microsoft
  account closure next of kin"); note in your folder that this is slower.
- **Facebook/Meta:** Settings → **Memorialization settings** — set legacy
  contact and/or request deletion.
- **Smaller services:** check each app's **Account settings → Security** or
  its help pages for "deceased user" (most small services don't have one —
  that's what your executor list is for).

If a service has no legacy feature: it goes on your executor list with the
word **D** or **M** next to it, and they handle it with a death certificate
(see [executor checklists](06-executor-checklists.md)).

## For executors: how to actually close or memorialize someone's account

Before you start, gather: **your ID, your proof of role (executor
certificate/court papers or next-of-kin status), and the death certificate**
copies. Then:

1. **Email:** contact the provider's help center, search their site for
   "deceased account" — each major provider has a form. Expect days, not
   hours. Don't delete anything until you've exported what matters (photos,
   documents, receipts).
2. **Social:** use each platform's deceased-user form (search "<platform>
   deceased user"). Memorialization is usually quick; deletion needs papers.
3. **Everything else:** a polite support email with paperwork attached works
   nearly everywhere human beings work. Note the date and name of who you
   spoke to, every time.
4. **Wrong-way protection:** never log into a deceased person's account
   without executor/estate standing if the will or the platform forbids it —
   even with the password, terms-of-service violations around deceased
   accounts can create real problems. Use the official channels; they exist
   for precisely this.

You're allowed to go slowly. Accounts do not expire if you take three months
of quiet before touching them.
