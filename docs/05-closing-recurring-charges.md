# Closing Recurring Charges for a Loved One

*Subscriptions are designed to keep charging forever — even after life
changes. This page is the playbook for stopping them: for someone who is
in the hospital, has died, or can no longer manage their own money. Work top
to bottom; the first three steps stop 90% of the bleeding.*

---

## Before you start (5 minutes)

You'll want close at hand:

- Their phone (a locked phone can still show recurring payments in a wallet
  app with a passcode you have access to; otherwise proceed without it)
- Any bank or card statement — paper or PDF — covering the last 3 months
- Your role papers if you have them (power of attorney, executor
  certificate); not required for steps 1–2 below

## Step 1 — Mine the statements (the master move)

The single most reliable way to find what's actually charging: **read the
statements.** Companies hide renewals in odd names ("SQ *PAP...", "APL*..."),
but they're all there.

1. Get the last **3 months** of statements for every card and bank account.
2. List every non-obvious recurring charge: streaming, cloud storage, news,
   gyms, apps, meal boxes, charitable pledges, insurance, rent utilities
   autopay.
3. For each, note: exact merchant name on the statement, the amount, the date
   it charges, and whether it's charged to a card or drawn from a bank (ACH).

Now you have the ground truth — no app needed, no guessing.

## Step 2 — Cut at the strongest point

Order matters. Cancel at whichever of these is strongest:

1. **The account itself** (their streaming account → cancel plan): cleanest.
   Needs their login → see [what never to
   share](03-what-never-to-share.md) for getting credentials honestly.
2. **The card** — call the number on the physical card, or the bank: report
   the circumstance (hospitalization or death) and ask for the card to be
   **closed or locked**. Recurring charges on a dead card simply stop.
   *(Careful if the card also pays needed bills like insurance — check
   Step 4's keep-list first.)*
3. **The bank (ACH autodrafts)** — tell the bank the account holder
   has died; a documented death stops ACH debits and the bank will handle
   mortgage/utility questions with you.
4. **The merchant** — the slow lane: call each merchant, ask for "account
   closed due to death; please cancel and stop billing." Get a name and a
   confirmation number each time.

## Step 3 — The keep-list (don't stop these by accident)

Some autopays keep lights on. Check before pulling the plug:

- Rent or mortgage, utilities, phone (people to notify depend on it)
- Insurance (life, health, home) — **grieving lapses in these are expensive
  mistakes**; if unsure, keep paying one month while you sort the rest
- Worthless-but-small: some families keep one streaming service running a
  while for shared watching; that's allowed comfort, not failure

## Death-specific notes (for the executor)

- **Joint accounts** you're on: recurring charges continue until *you* cancel
  them — nobody automatically cancels anything, ever, for any reason.
- **Individual accounts:** tell merchants and banks the account holder died;
  most mortgages/similar come with a 2–4 week grace period before hitting
  you with late fees. Ask. "What's the grace period in my situation?" is a
  completely normal question.
- **Refunds:** you can politely ask for pro-rated refunds on annual plans;
  sometimes they're granted, often not — it's a bonus, not a fight.
- **Final card statements** keep arriving as merchants catch up; that's fine
  and expected. That's what the estate pays from.
- Keep everything in one place: confirmations, dates, names. Add them to the
  [executor checklists](06-executor-checklists.md) folder.
